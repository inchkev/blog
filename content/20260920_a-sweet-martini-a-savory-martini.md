## Sweet: Herbal lemon drop martini

- 2 oz fennel infused gin
- 1 oz lemon juice
- 1 oz fennel simple syrup
- 0.2 oz [Brucato Chaparral Amaro](https://brucatospirits.com/products/chaparral)
- 0.2 oz Cointreau
- 0.4 oz manzanilla sherry

Shake with ice, strain, serve in a chilled martini glass. Garnish with a fennel frond.

### Details

- **Fennel infused gin**: I followed [this recipe](https://www.liquor.com/fennelcello-recipe-5120764) for “fennelcello” but I used gin (hendrick’s) instead of vodka and the final result didn’t taste very strongly of fennel.
- **Fennel syrup**: I added an equal weight of sugar to a finely diced fennel bulb including the fronds. Let that sit overnight, then strain through a cheese cloth. Honestly, this didn’t give me intense anise-y fennel flavor I wanted. I’ll try a simmered syrup with star anise in the future.
- I need to try Italian finocchietto liquer or try [making my own](https://www.cucicucicoo.com/2017/07/homemade-wild-fennel-liqueur-recipe-finocchietto-digestif/).

## Savory: Tomato martini

- 2 oz vodka
- 2 oz tomato water (see details)
- 0.6 oz [Veso Tomato Vermouth](https://drinkveso.com/products/tomato-vermouth?variant=46942543708317)
- 0.4 oz vermouth bianco

### Details

- **Tomato water**: Use the ripest in-season tomatoes you can find. Prefer ones with high sugar and glutamate content, such as early girls, heirlooms, or sweet cherry tomatoes.
	- Rough dice and remove the cores but do not remove the seeds (that is where the flavor is).
	- By weight, add 1% salt, 1.5% shiro/white miso, 0.25% light soy sauce (white soy sauce also works).
	- Mash until a lot of liquid has been released.
	- Add additional salt, msg, and red wine vinegar to balance. Add only small amounts at a time. This is only really needed when the tomatoes you are using are not the ripest.
- I used [Bordiga Bianco Vermouth](https://www.bittersandbottles.com/products/bordiga-bianco-vermouth), it is so good.
